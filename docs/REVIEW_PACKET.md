# EyesClose: review packet

This file is made by `npm run review-packet` from the files in `content/`. Do not edit it by hand.

## What EyesClose is

EyesClose is a free website for families in India after a death. A family member answers about ten questions.
The site then shows a checklist of what to close or claim, who to contact, and a link to each official page.
It also makes three letters to print, and has pages for harder situations.

Answers stay on the phone. Nothing typed is sent to us or stored.

## What it does not do

- It is general information, not legal advice.
- It does not say who inherits what. Those rules differ by religion and by state.
- It does not contact anyone or file anything for the family.
- A letter goes with the institution's own form. It does not replace it.
- Nothing has been checked by a CA or lawyer yet. Every item shows "Not yet checked by a professional".

## What we ask you

1. Is anything here wrong?
2. Is anything missing that would mislead a family?
3. Are the "When to talk to a lawyer" notes right?

Each row below is one claim as it appears in the app, with the official page it comes from and the date we
checked that page. "What the source says" is our note of the source's own words. Please mark any row you disagree with.

## Our suggestions

These are marked in the app as our own suggestion, not from an official page.

- Help: If the death certificate is not ready. Ask the registrar's office what they need for a late registration, and what the fee is. Rules and fees can differ by state.
- Help: If the death certificate is not ready. You can start the steps on your checklist that do not need the certificate.
- Help: If the death certificate is not ready. When the certificate is ready, get a few copies. Many places want to keep one.
- Help: If there is no nominee. Ask the bank or insurer for its claim form and list of papers. Your checklist links to each bank's own page.
- Help: If there is no nominee. If the nominee has also died, tell the bank or insurer and ask in writing what they need from you.
- Help: Legal heir and succession certificates. Ask each bank or insurer which paper they will accept before you apply for one.
- Help: Legal heir and succession certificates. Ask your taluk, tahsil or revenue office who issues legal heir certificates where you live. This differs from place to place.
- Help: Legal heir and succession certificates. Keep looking for a will in their papers, and ask any lawyer they used.
- Help: If the account was joint. Look at the passbook or the account opening form for words like "either or survivor". Ask the bank if you are not sure.
- Help: If the account was joint. Tell the bank about the death before anyone uses the account.
- Help: If the account was joint. If the account had only one holder and no nominee, read the page "If there is no nominee".
- Help: If the death happened outside India. Contact the Indian embassy or consulate in that country first. Its website lists who to call.
- Help: If the death happened outside India. Ask for several copies of the death certificate from that country, with an English translation if it is in another language. Banks and insurers in India ask for proof of death.
- Help: If police or a post-mortem are involved. Ask the police station handling the case how to get copies of the FIR, the inquest report and the post-mortem report. Insurers and banks may ask for them.
- Help: If police or a post-mortem are involved. Make sure the death is registered. The page "If the death certificate is not ready" explains how.
- Help: If police or a post-mortem are involved. It is fine to ask a friend or relative to go to the police station with you.
- Checklist: Find their bank accounts. Look for passbooks, cheque books, bank letters and bank messages on their phone. Then tell each bank about the death.
- Checklist: Crypto and online investments. Do not log in to their accounts. Make a list of the apps, emails and papers that mention them, and talk to a professional before anything is moved.
- Checklist: Get the death certificate. Most steps on this list need a copy of the death certificate.
- Checklist: Provident fund and EPS pension (EPFO). Their last employer's HR team may be able to help with the claim.
- Checklist: Central government pension. State pensions may differ, so contact the pension office.
- Checklist: Health insurance. Tell the insurer about the death. If others in the family are on the same policy, ask how their cover continues.
- Checklist: Loans and credit cards. Tell each card company and lender about the death and ask how they handle the dues. We have not found an official page for loans yet.
- Checklist: Other email and social accounts. Each service has its own steps. Look in its help pages for what to do after a death.
- Checklist: Airtel mobile number. Ask at an Airtel store how to close the number or keep it in the family, and which papers to bring.
- Checklist: BSNL mobile number. Ask at a BSNL customer service centre how to close the number or keep it in the family, and which papers to bring.
- Checklist: Their other mobile number. Check which company the number is with. Then ask at its store how to close the number or keep it in the family.
- Checklist: Amazon, if they had it. Save any photos or files you want to keep before asking for closure.
- Checklist: Other subscriptions. Look at bank and card statements for regular payments. Then cancel each one with the company.
- Checklist: Google Pay. UPI IDs stay linked to a bank account, so also tell the bank.
- Checklist: Other UPI or wallet apps. Contact each app's support team, tell them about the death, and ask which papers they need.
- Checklist: Paytm. Contact Paytm support, tell them about the death, and ask which papers they need. If they had money in a Paytm wallet, ask how the family can claim it.
- Checklist: PhonePe. Contact PhonePe support, tell them about the death, and ask which papers they need. The money itself is in the linked bank account, so the bank step matters most.
- Checklist: Vehicle and its insurance. Also tell the vehicle insurer.

## When to talk to a lawyer

**If the death certificate is not ready**

- The registrar will not register the death, or asks for a magistrate's or court order.
- Details on the certificate are wrong, and the office will not correct them.
- Family members disagree about the date, place or cause of death.

**If there is no nominee**

- More than one person claims the money, or the heirs do not agree.
- The amount is above the bank's limit and you are asked for a succession certificate.
- One of the heirs is a child under 18.
- There is money or property in more than one state.

**Legal heir and succession certificates**

- Who inherits can depend on religion, on the state, and on whether there is a will. A lawyer can tell you what applies to your family.
- Heirs disagree, or someone says there is a will that others have not seen.
- A child under 18 is one of the heirs.
- There is land or a house, or property in more than one state.

**If the account was joint**

- The surviving holder and the legal heirs do not agree about the money.
- The way the account was opened is unclear, and the bank asks for court papers.
- A child under 18 is a holder or an heir.

**If the death happened outside India**

- Police in that country are investigating the death.
- There is money or property in that country as well as in India.
- The family does not agree about burial, cremation or bringing the body home.

**If police or a post-mortem are involved**

- You think someone caused the death, or you disagree with the police report.
- There is a claim for compensation after a road accident.
- An insurer refuses a claim because of how the death happened.
